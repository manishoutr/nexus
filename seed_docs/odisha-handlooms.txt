Handloom Traditions of Odisha

Odisha has a long handloom tradition centred on the ikat technique, known
locally as bandha. In ikat weaving, the threads are tie-dyed before weaving,
so the pattern is created in the yarn itself rather than printed on finished
cloth.

Sambalpuri Textiles
The Sambalpuri saree is the best-known product of this tradition. It is
woven in the districts around Sambalpur, Bargarh and Sonepur in western
Odisha. Common motifs include the shankha or conch shell, the chakra or
wheel, and the phula or flower, all drawn from the iconography of the
Jagannath culture.

Weaving Communities
The Bhulia and Meher communities are traditional weavers of the region.
A single Sambalpuri saree can take one to two weeks to weave by hand,
depending on the complexity of the pattern. Both the warp and the weft may
be tie-dyed, in which case the weave is called double ikat.

Geographical Indication
Sambalpuri bandha textiles hold a Geographical Indication tag, which
recognises their link to the region and protects the name against
machine-made imitations sold under the same label.

Modern Market
Weaver cooperatives and state emporiums sell handloom products, and online
marketplaces have opened new channels. Designers now work with weavers to
adapt traditional motifs to contemporary garments while keeping the
handwoven process intact.
