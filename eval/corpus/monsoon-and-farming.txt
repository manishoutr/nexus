Monsoon and farming in Odisha

Most of the rain in Odisha arrives between June and September with the south-west monsoon. Farmers sow the kharif crop at its start, and rice is the main one. Fields that depend only on rain are called rainfed, and they suffer when the monsoon is late or breaks for weeks.

Cyclones from the Bay of Bengal sometimes arrive in October and November, close to harvest. Embankments, early warnings and shelters have reduced deaths from them, but standing crops can still be lost to flooding and salt water.

The rabi season follows in winter and uses irrigation from canals and tube wells. Pulses, oilseeds and vegetables are common rabi crops. Crop insurance and minimum support prices are central government schemes that farmers can use to lower the risk.
